# The Chronicle of the Kingmaker's War
*Being a true account of how Richard Neville, lord of the Isle of Man, made
himself first a crown-giver and then a king — set down from the game
chronicle by the house scribe, in the manner of Crowland.*

**In the first year of the war** the Bear stirred little, and men thought him
idle. He left his claim upon Middleham, that the North should remember whose
name it bore, and let a raid gutter out upon the Manx Sea as a candle before
sleep. The wise noted only this: he wasted nothing.

**In the second year the Crown itself was contested at Parliament**, and when
the sealed bids were opened it was the Bear who stood first upon the track,
and the Crown Token passed to House Neville — *and never afterward left it.*
Thus was the third mark achieved before the second year was old: he ordered
the realm as its sovereign, and where houses tied, it was Neville who told
them their places. In that same Parliament the Scots came over the Tweed in
strength, and by the sovereign's word it was Percy — Percy, keeper of those
very marches — whose knights fed the reivers at Alnwick. Some called this
judgment. The Bear called it housekeeping. Then he fell upon Lancaster in
the Irish Sea, five strengths against one, and the Rose fled routed to the
Firth of Forth.

**In the third year** he swept Percy from the Celtic Sea as he had swept
Lancaster, while in the east the Dragon dashed itself against the Sun and
learned what the Narrow Sea costs.

**In the fourth year the design was revealed.** At Parliament the Bear took
not only the Crown again but the Sword of State — crown in one paw, blade in
the other — and, as sovereign, ordered the Marshalcy with himself at its
head. Mark this well, for upon that quiet clerkly act the whole war would
turn. Then he pillaged the Rose's Livery, broke Percy at the Channel
Approaches, and stormed Alnwick itself, eight strengths against five, until
the garrison of the Lion's own seat was no more and the bear and ragged
staff flew over Percy's hearth. The second mark, achieved; the feud, settled;
and from the fifth year it was *Alnwick that mustered Neville's soldiers.*

*(Here the chronicle suffers a lacuna: the record itself froze mid-decision,
and the war was resurrected whole from the exported memory of it — a
miracle the scribes attribute to good export discipline. Let all houses
learn from it.)*

**In the fifth year came the reckoning.** Dover fell, nine against four, and
enemy carracks burned in its port. The Swan came against him in the Celtic
Sea and was thrown back. Then Percy, seeking vengeance, met him at the
Channel Approaches — **four against four, a dead tie — and the tie fell to
the higher upon the Marshalcy, which was Neville, because Neville had
ordered it so.** And at the last he came before the city of Lancaster
itself, and the battle stood **ten against ten — tied again — and again the
track he had written gave him the day.** The garrison of Lancaster was no
more; the Rose fled routed to St Albans; her carracks burned at her own
docks. Then the Bear marched from the Isle of Man one final time, took his
seventh seat, and the war ended at once.

**Final standings:** 🐻 7 · ☀ 3 · 🦢 2 · 🦁 2 · 🐉 2 · 🌹 1.

*Let it be recorded plainly, that none mistake the lesson: the Kingmaker won
two battles he did not win on the field. He won them in Parliament, in the
fourth year, with a clerk's pen — for kings are made by stronger men than
kings, and this one made himself.*

— **Glory-marks:** 🌊 the seas held · 🐻 Middleham kept and Alnwick taken ·
👑 sovereign from the second year, and king at the end. **All three.**
